The Stars In Our Skies

This program will show you different kinds of Mollweide projection pertaining to stars in our celestial sphere!


What is a Mollweide projection?...

A Mollweide projection is an equal area map that can show spherical coordinates on a 2D plane while preserving accurate proportions.


What is the celestial sphere?...

The celestial sphere is an imaginary, infinitely large hollow globe surrounding us. The earth is assumed to be stationary at the center of this sphere. When you look up at the might sky, you see half, or 180 degrees of the full celstial sphere.


How do we 'graph' stars onto this sphere?...

Every star in the celestial sphere has a right ascention and declination. They are similar to the latitude and longitude coordinate system used on Earth. Right ascention (longitude) measures how east from the vernal equinox a star is. This dataset measures it in degrees (0-360), but some also measure right ascention in seconds, minutes, and hours. Delcination (latitude) measures the angle of a stars north or south direction. It is measured from -90 to 90, with a full -90 meaning the star is at the south celestial pole and vice versa.

This project seems so cool! Where did you get the data from to do this?...

The dataset used in this project comes from the Gaia Data Release 3, a catalog that contains information about 1.8 billion objects in our universe observed by the European Space Agencie's Gaia satellite. However, I only downloaded a random sample of 50,000 stars for this project, but I hope to harness the catalog's full potential in the near future!
